# Community Feedback — What Makes Zombies Quests Feel Good or Bad

Updated: 2026-09-20

Purpose: preserve recurring player feedback about quest design. Community comments are subjective, but repeated themes are useful when designing original Xziel maps.

## 1. Steps feel best when they belong to the map's theme

Recent community discussion repeatedly praises Ancient Evil because the quest steps feel tied to Greek mythology, theatre and the map's story instead of feeling like disconnected chores. Players specifically praise the sense that each step has a reason to exist.

Xziel rule:
Every major quest step must answer:
**Why would this action make sense in this location, with this story and this map mechanic?**

If the answer is only "because Easter Egg," redesign it.

## 2. Map evolution creates memorable progression

Players often praise Der Eisendrache and Ancient Evil because the map changes as the quest progresses: new weapons, rooms, rituals, story beats and boss access.

Xziel rule:
A Main Quest should visibly change at least 3 things:
- environment;
- available traversal;
- NPC/companion behavior;
- sky/weather;
- enemy roster;
- music;
- machinery;
- safehouse/portal state.

The map should not look exactly the same at minute 55 as it did at minute 5.

## 3. Active steps beat end-of-round chores

Gorod Krovi is often praised for challenges and lockdowns that keep players actively fighting rather than spending long stretches saving one zombie while reading a guide.

Xziel rule:
Favor:
- combat puzzles;
- escorts;
- moving objectives;
- short timed deductions;
- in-combat interactions.

Use "hold the last zombie" only as a convenience, never as the intended core loop.

## 4. Setup should give useful toys even if the player abandons the quest

Origins remains loved partly because staff construction gives immediately fun weapons. Players can start a quest, stop halfway and still have gained useful equipment.

Xziel rule:
Every major setup chain should pay out along the way.

Bad:
collect 12 objects → no reward until minute 50.

Good:
- part 1 opens shortcut;
- part 2 gives weapon;
- part 3 upgrades weapon;
- full chain opens finale.

## 5. Quest mechanics should be fun on repeat runs

Community praise for Director's Cut often centers on removing tedious setup from future casual runs.

Xziel rule:
After a player has proven mastery, consider optional shortcuts:
- remembered code category;
- permanent fast-start mode;
- alternate build route;
- cosmetic shortcut;
- accelerated setup toggle.

But keep Classic Mode available so earned bonuses do not remove progression for players who enjoy it.

## 6. RNG is tolerated when bounded; hated when it controls progress

Moon is a common frustration example because players can spend huge point totals waiting for required Mystery Box items and may also wait on specific excavator behavior.

Xziel rule:
Never gate the Main Quest behind unbounded RNG.

Required RNG needs:
- pity counter;
- maximum attempt count;
- alternate deterministic route;
- guaranteed spawn after threshold;
- visible progress toward guarantee.

A required quest tool must always become obtainable eventually.

## 7. Four-player locks age badly

Older maps such as original Ascension, Die Rise, Shangri-La and some BO2 endings are frequently discussed as frustrating because the quest requires a specific player count or synchronization structure.

Xziel rule:
Every Main Quest ships with:
- solo route;
- 2-player route;
- 3-player route;
- 4-player route.

Simultaneous-input translation:
- co-op: players stand on plates together;
- solo: activate plates in a timed sequence or command a companion to hold one.

Do not lock story completion behind matchmaking population.

## 8. "Pull up a video/spreadsheet" is a warning sign

Blood of the Dead and some staff-style puzzles receive criticism when the logic is difficult to infer from the map itself, especially when players feel forced to pause and consult an external guide.

Xziel rule:
External guides may make a puzzle easier, but should never be the only realistic way to understand it.

Use:
- visual legend nearby;
- recoverable notes;
- quest journal recording discovered evidence;
- NPC clue after repeated failure;
- optional Guided mode.

## 9. Running across a huge map is not difficulty

Players criticize steps that repeatedly send them from one extreme of a large map to another without introducing a new challenge.

Xziel rule:
Long traversal must have one of these:
- vehicle;
- fast travel;
- combat event;
- changing environment;
- timed threat;
- meaningful route choice;
- collectible opportunity.

Otherwise it is dead time.

## 10. Repeated soul boxes need variation

Kill-near-object is one of Zombies' most common quest primitives, but community fatigue appears when a quest overuses it.

Xziel rule:
If a map has several charge objectives, change at least one variable each time:
- moving target;
- different required enemy;
- elemental damage;
- no leaving zone;
- protect object;
- object must be carried;
- kills must be airborne/headshots/trap kills;
- charge competes against drain.

Avoid six identical circles requiring 30 kills each.

## 11. Boss fights make completion feel more final

Community discussion often favors quests with a clear boss encounter over older quests that simply end after a puzzle sequence.

Xziel rule:
Large Main Quests should usually end with one of:
- boss;
- escape;
- defense;
- branch confrontation;
- survival event.

A cinematic alone should not carry all the finale weight.

## 12. Bosses should be mechanically legible

Players tend to remember good bosses when weak points, phase rules and previous map mechanics carry into the fight.

Xziel boss rule:
- clear telegraph;
- readable weak point;
- no invisible immunity;
- resources between phases;
- no unexplained instant wipe;
- checkpoint/retry policy documented by the game;
- map's signature mechanic matters.

## 13. Surprise genre shifts are memorable

Community-favorite side Easter eggs include:
- coffin dance;
- nightclub;
- arcade machines;
- delivery routes;
- murder mystery;
- rhythm/dance events;
- chess/logic puzzles.

Xziel rule:
Each large map should contain one optional secret where the game briefly stops feeling like normal Zombies.

That surprise is part of the identity players talk about afterward.

## 14. Reward power should be optional

Some players love permanent starting power such as Cold War's weapon-rarity reward; others feel it removes the sense of progression. Director's Cut is widely appreciated partly because it is a toggle.

Xziel rule:
Permanent gameplay bonuses must be selectable.

Suggested presets:
- Classic Start — no persistent power.
- Earned Start — enabled unlocked bonuses.
- Cursed Start — harder rules, higher prestige rewards.

## 15. Reward should match effort

A 90-minute quest ending in only XP or a calling card can feel weak.

Reward ladder:
- short secret → ammo, points, temporary buff;
- 10-minute side quest → perk / weapon / unique utility;
- major side quest → relic / companion upgrade / cosmetic;
- Main Quest → ending + persistent token + meaningful in-run reward;
- Super Quest → mode-changing unlock + prestigious cosmetic.

## 16. Side quests should stay optional

Origins demonstrates both sides of this lesson: its side systems are deeply satisfying, but some players feel the map's best equipment is so tied to quest-like setup that casual survival becomes dominated by Easter Egg chores.

Xziel rule:
A player who wants to ignore the Main Quest must still have:
- viable weapons;
- accessible Pack-a-Punch;
- basic perks;
- at least one strong training/camping route;
- normal survival progression.

Quest participation should enrich the map, not become mandatory homework.

## 17. Repetition should create mastery, not annoyance

A quest can be replayable when the player gets faster and smarter.

Good repeatability:
- known routes;
- optional random clue;
- multiple branches;
- skill-based time save;
- alternate Wonder Weapon;
- different side objective.

Bad repeatability:
- same 20-item fetch route;
- same unskippable dialogue;
- same long forced escort;
- same lottery-box grind.

## 18. Community-scale mysteries still matter

Even when rewards are small, players remember secrets that generated community discussion.

Xziel should deliberately include:
- launch-day unsolved clues;
- layered cyphers;
- one cross-map mystery;
- one environmental clue that points to a future map;
- optional developer riddles.

But the Main Quest itself should remain solvable without data mining.

## 19. Design scorecard for every Xziel quest step

Before shipping a step, answer 0/1:

- Fits map theme?
- Gives clear feedback?
- Understandable from in-world evidence?
- Solo-safe?
- Bounded RNG?
- Retryable?
- Adds active gameplay?
- Pays off within reasonable time?
- Uses a mechanic learned earlier?
- Fun on the fifth replay?

Target: 8/10 or better.

Any step scoring 5/10 or lower should be redesigned.

## 20. Summary

The recurring community preference is not simply "easy" versus "hard."

Players respond well to quests that are:
- cohesive;
- active;
- readable;
- thematic;
- rewarding;
- replayable;
- solo-capable;
- mechanically varied.

They become frustrated by:
- arbitrary interactions;
- unbounded RNG;
- mandatory player counts;
- guide dependence;
- excessive dead travel;
- repetitive soul charging;
- unrewarding long setup.

For Xziel, difficulty should come from **execution, pressure, deduction and mastery**, not from obscurity or wasting the player's time.
